The Story of Gilgamesh is one of the oldest known works of literature and some parts might be lost to time. The prologue declares that Gilgamesh, the ancient king of Uruk, took a long journey and brought back the story of the flood. Descended from the gods, he gave the city strong walls and built magnificent temples. In the Second tablet Gilgamesh grows arrogant, so the gods create Enkidu to challenge him. Enkidu is a wild man, living among animals, until he is seduced by a prostitute who lures him to civilization. He then lives with the shepherds and defends the flocks. Gilgamesh begins to have dreams about the coming of Enkidu, and his mother interprets them to mean that the gods are sending a friend. Enkidu travels to the city to confront Gilgamesh’s tyranny. Enkidu loses the fight, but immediately becomes Gilgamesh’s best friend. The Third Tablet is when Gilgamesh and his arrogance starts to to get the better of him and it starts of with Gilgamesh having a dream, and is sad because it relates that he can never be immortal. Meanwhile, Enkidu is growing tired of city life. The two friends decide to go and fight Humbaba, the giant who protects the sacred cedar forest. They ask for and gain the sun god Shamash’s protection. After a long journey, they lure the giant out of the forest by chopping down one of his trees. They fight, defeat him, and take cedars from the forest. However, by killing Humbaba, they anger the god Enlil.
